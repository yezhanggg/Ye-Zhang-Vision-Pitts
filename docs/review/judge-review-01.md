# Judge review 01 — VisionPitts, Track 3 (Housing Typology, Equity & Climate Matchmaker)

Reviewer: senior Pittsburgh housing planner (City Planning, a CDC, a PHFA-funded developer), Track 3 judge, 8 minutes per team.
Reviewed Sat 2026-09-26, 19:00–20:30 ET, on commit `2b2a1f1` (dev server, `export/index.html` over `file://`, the public Vercel build), with a smoke pass on the uncommitted layout refactor that landed at 18:35 ET (floating panels, header chips; same numbers, same findings). Three sub-reviews feed this page: `_agentA-product.md` (product), `_agentB-method-data.md` (method and data), `_agentC-architecture.md` (engineering); captures are in `shots/`. Every number below was read off the screen, the shipped JSON, or the engine. Nothing in the product was changed for this review.

**The one good line:** the Match tract panel (answer card, six factor cards with raw value, source, year and confidence, a per-tract data-limits list) plus the Compare-scenarios flip map is the most honest, application-ready tract read I have seen in a hackathon tool, and it meets the Track 3 brief as written: two or more scenarios for a real place, adjustable normative weights, evidence and values labeled. Everything below is about what keeps it from winning.

## Part 1 — First 60 seconds

- Landing (`shots/A1-landing.png`): a live 3D map, "Great decisions need vision. We give you one.", four tiles. Nothing on the page states the question the tool answers: *which of five housing types, for renters at or below 50% AMI, without displacement, in which Pittsburgh tract*. The primary tile reads "Browse the census, then match housing types to places". The census browser is billed first, the Track 3 tool second.
- First click, "Open VisionPitts", starts a globe flight with a title card; the Skip button stays on screen until 13.4 s (`A2b-intro-6s.png`). It lands on Explore, a census browser ("Browse the census, one shape at a time"). The matchmaker is behind the header pill "Analysis" or a small "Analysis →" button.
- The first rewarding click is four actions away: Open → Skip → Analysis → a demo chip. On an 8-minute clock two minutes go to the intro and the browser. Once on Match with Hazelwood ("Small apartment · 65 / 100 match · Likely pick. Stays #1 in 6 of 10", `E1-match-hazelwood.png`) the product is immediately legible. That screen should be the first door.
- Where I got lost: on Explore I looked for "the score" in the Data panel (37 ACS variables, none scored) and found nothing that says Analysis is the matchmaker.
- What the first minute costs in engineering terms (Agent C, measured): after Open, the rail paints only when the map is ready, 1.1–2.6 s on a GPU and 3.5–5.3 s on software rendering, because map construction runs synchronously in the click's effect. Then the flight: 2.2 s spin + 5 s fly. Then 5–16 MB of tiles.

## Part 3 — Method critique and the three-tract check

**Structural findings (these matter more than any cell).**
1. **The subsidy flag is a switch, not a factor.** Balanced winners by `subsidy_eligible`: flag = 0 (36 tracts) → townhome 28, ADU 8, senior 0, small apt 0; flag = 1 (78 tracts) → senior 40, small apt 35, townhome 3, ADU 0. A 0/1 input with fits 1.0 / 0.8 against 0.0–0.2 decides the family of answer; the other five factors only choose within it. Halving the fits does not fix it (senior still 42). The flag must be graded (0 / 0.5 CDBG- or OZ-only / 1.0 QCT or SDDA).
2. **Senior wins because nothing measures seniors.** The senior row (`need .6, market 0, displacement .2, subsidy .8, transit .6, flood −1`) is the least demanding row, so it is the default wherever the flag is on and the market is weak (24 of 38 weak-tercile tracts). Both remedies in `docs/assumptions.md` §2 fail when run: senior market 0.15 → 40 → 37; senior flood −0.5 → **57** (worse). A senior-demand signal that already sits in the bundle (`acs_tract.json` `age65_share`, percentile-ranked, fit senior 0.8) gives senior 30, small apt 44, ADU 9, townhome 31. Caveat: North Oakland reads 45% 65+ (a facility tract).
3. **Duplex/triplex can never win** (0 of 114; a generous row `subsidy .5, market .2, need .7` still gives 0). It is strongest on things the tool does not measure: by-right zoning (R2/R3/RM), vacant and city-owned lots, existing 2–4 unit stock (`units_2_4_share` is in the Explore bundle but not scored). The typology the land bank and the CDCs actually build is structurally excluded.
4. **`need` is student-dominated at the top**: North Oakland 1,005, South Oakland 855, Central Oakland 835 ≤50%-AMI renter households. South Oakland (need pct .99) gets senior housing. An undergraduate-enrollment flag (ACS B14007) is a 30-minute fix.
5. **Transit per land acre penalizes brownfield tracts.** Hazelwood 5623: 6,580 weekday departures on ~770 acres → 8.5/acre → pct .25; Squirrel Hill North 5,826 on ~233 acres → pct .61. Per household the order reverses (Hazelwood .60, SqHN .54, Homewood North .82). Normalize by households or by units within 400 m of a stop.
6. **Flood is the least credible factor and carries the strongest aversions (−1).** `flood_share_pct`: Shadyside 42003070500 79.6%, Homewood South 75.5%, Homewood North 31.8%, all tagged high confidence in 114/114 tracts. No resident recognizes those numbers. FEMA NFHL is in the organizers' catalog.
7. **"Anti-displacement" is mis-named for what it does.** In Squirrel Hill North, tripling the displacement weight makes *low* risk the largest contributor to townhome (0.317 of 0.669). "Build market-rate where nobody is displaced" is defensible; "Protect current residents first" (`PRESET_COPY`) is not what the weights do. F-1 already says so in the docs; say it on screen.

**Fit-matrix cells I would change** (`config/scoring.json` v0.3.0; effect tested with the shipped engine): small apt × subsidy 1.0 → 0.6 and senior × subsidy 0.8 → 0.6 (with the graded flag); senior × market 0 → 0.15 (a senior LIHTC deal still needs a market study and a lender); senior × need 0.6 → 0.3 (`need` is all-age family demand); senior × transit 0.6 → 0.8 (R2 says seniors depend on transit); small apt × flood −0.5 → −0.8 (F-4's "elevated ground floors" is not how a walk-up is underwritten, and R10 is backwards: the flat commercial lots on Second Ave and Carson St *are* the flood land, South Side Flats 68.7%); townhome × need 0.2 → 0 (a market townhome serves no ≤50%-AMI renter without IZ or the bonus); townhome × displacement −0.6 → −0.4; duplex × subsidy / market 0.2 / 0.4 → 0.5 / 0.2 (scattered-site infill is subsidized and is the weak-market product; still 0 wins, see finding 3); senior × flood −1 keep (do not take the docs' −0.5).

**Rulebook (R1–R12) and the two unruled cells.** R7 lumps small apartment (.4) and senior (.2) under "gentle density"; a 5–19 unit building is not gentle density and does not add units without removing anyone on an occupied site. R10's rationale is inverted. R11 sets cells for a factor that does not exist in v0.3.0. Write the two missing rules rather than zeroing the cells (zeroing changes 19 Balanced picks as a side effect): "an ADU carries no land cost, so it is the one market unit that can rent near 50% AMI: need .3"; "a 5–19 unit building carries a first mortgage sized on market rents even with LIHTC: market .3". Doc drift a judge will catch: eligible tracts 73 (docs §2) / 82 (handoff F-3) / 78 in the data; watch list 9 (docs, app) vs 12 (handoff C-8); the FY2026 2BR FMR $1,299 drives `rent_2br_gt_fmr` but has no `sources.md` row.

**Is the data-versus-values line legible on screen?** Yes at the frame level ("Your values" pill, Sources modal "Observed data" vs "Fit rules: a value judgment", the fit matrix printed with numbers, the last bullet of the data-limits panel). No at the point of decision: the answer card says "Small apartment · 74 / 100 match · Why: many low-income renters · subsidy eligibility"; the first phrase is an observation, the second a fit rule, and nothing marks the difference. The fit matrix is visible only inside Sources and cannot be edited in the UI (B11 unshipped). Fix is copy: "74 / 100 fit under *your* priorities", "Why (fit rule): …", legend "Best match under your priorities".

**Would the uncertainty shown change what a planner does? Mostly not, for four reasons.** (1) Three of six tags are constant: `displacement_risk_conf` low in 114/114 (R-5), transit and flood high in 114/114; a tag that says the same thing everywhere is ignored after the second tract. (2) Stability perturbs weights only; data uncertainty never reaches the pick (42 tracts with need CV > 30% get the same "Solid pick"). (3) "Stays #1 in N of 10" at 200 draws has a standard error of about a third of a step: the engines agree (Python .45, TS .455) but the app shows 6 of 10 for Hazelwood; the parity fixture has no stability cases. (4) 49 of 114 tracts have a #1–#2 margin under 0.05 (23 under 0.02) and the "Which type wins" map paints all of them solid. A hatch for close calls would change what goes to a meeting; nothing else in the panel would.

**T-3 (ADU by right × 1.5):** confirmed a no-op, 0 flips. The proposed score multiplier (×1.2 → 28 flips, ×1.4 → 77) is a knob with no data behind it; do not ship it. By-right only means something relative to the other four types being conditional or prohibited in the same district; that is a per-typology gate from the zoning map, not a fit change.

- **Hazelwood: half-defensible.** "Small apartment on Second Avenue, senior a close second, a watch-list tract with +0.31 market pressure from Greenfield and Squirrel Hill" is a sentence I could say at a Hazelwood Initiative meeting. What I could not answer: *where* (no vacant-lot, city-owned or land-bank count; Hazelwood has hundreds of lots and a CLT), *why not the lots* (duplex and ADU infill is the neighborhood plan's answer and the tool cannot rank a mix), *why transit reads worse than 75% of the city* (the per-acre artifact), and *how a 5–19 unit building protects anyone* (the preset returns a building, not a protection; the watch list and pressure layer carry that story and are the strongest part of the demo). Hazelwood Green sits inside the tract and drives both the flood share and the acreage.
- **Homewood North: not defensible as stated.** Weakest market in the city (raw .029): a 5–19 unit new build does not pencil at that scale; the real products are rehab of the 2–4 unit stock, scattered-site infill on land-bank lots and, yes, a larger senior or family LIHTC building on an assembled site. The tool's #1 is the wrong scale and its #3 (duplex .480) is the right one but can never win. Then "32% of the land floods": Homewood has stormwater trouble, not a floodplain a resident recognizes; that number ends the meeting.
- **Squirrel Hill North: defensible as a market statement, not as an affordability one.** "Townhome 72, ADU 61, 10 of 10" is stable and true to the MVA, but there is no subsidy designation, no IZ overlay here, and a market townhome serves no ≤50%-AMI renter. The only sub-market unit that gets built here is an ADU on a large R1 lot, by right since the 2025 package, and the tool ranks it second because townhome × market is 1.0 and ADU × market 0.5. The audience will also ask why "displacement risk low" when the CHAS burden share among ≤50%-AMI renters is .79.

## Part 5 — Architecture and the Supabase verdict (condensed from Agent C)

- **Flow:** raw → `scripts/01–08` → parquet/CSV → `export.write_all()` writes 19 JSON files (3.74 MB) into `app/src/data/` → inlined at build with `import.meta.glob(?raw)` → zustand state → memoized scoring per weights object → `setFeatureState` into MapLibre. The export is the same bundle in one file. Good: one honest data contract (`TractProps` equals what `properties()` writes), a pure engine mirrored in Python with a 40-case fixture, a URL hash that round-trips the whole state (10 tests), and a bundled-first / Supabase-swap loader that fits a double-click file.
- **Bundle** (measured): JS 5,400,282 B raw / 983,045 B gzip; `export/index.html` 5,519,532 B. Top five: `buildings.json` 2,473,909 B (45.8%, 9,212 footprints in 8 demo tracts), `maplibre-gl` 1,055,221 (19.5%), `acs_bg.json` 422,223 (7.8%), `tracts.json` 275,595 (5.1%, properties 77% of it), `react-dom` 210,811 (3.9%). Data is 69% of the file, packages 27%, app source 3.6%.
- **Startup** (Playwright, 1440×900): the file parses in ~0.25 s ("Open" visible at 335–377 ms). What the judge waits for is WebGL plus basemap and DEM fetch plus the 7.2 s flight plus 5–16 MB of tiles: Explore map ready 3.5–3.7 s on software rendering, 0.58 s on a GPU; idle at 9–11 s (5.5 s GPU). The dev server is 15× slower to "Layers" (3.8 s); never demo from it.
- **Brittle:** `MapView.tsx` (891 lines) owns intro, terrain, contours, overlays, sync, pins, orbit; three instances live per session, each re-sending the 2.4 MB buildings to its own worker. `AnimatePresence mode="wait"` destroys and rebuilds the map on every Explore↔Analysis switch (1.0–4.7 s measured). After Open, the rail waits behind a synchronous `new maplibregl.Map` (1–5 s frozen fade). A WebGL failure leaves "Loading map…" forever (unhandled rejection). `remote.ts` poisons the whole session on the first failed request, and county block groups already need two PostgREST pages against an 8 s budget. A missing or unparsable JSON ships as a silently empty app. `LAYER_ROWS` counts and `app/vercel.json` `/data/` header are stale.
- **Scale:** scoring and paint are fine at 2× (114 × 5 × 6 per weights object). Buildings break first: 46% of the file for 8 tracts, ≈40 MB for 128 (estimate). Then PostgREST paging with jsonb geometry. Then `useGeo` fetching all five levels' county geometry on mount whether or not the layer is on.
- **Supabase today:** `acs_variables`, `geo_units` (jsonb geometry), `acs_values` (60,236 rows), `dataset_versions`, public-select RLS, written only by `scripts/08_publish_supabase.py` with the secret key. Explore reads county rows when online and falls back to the bundle; Analysis is fully client-side; `scenarios` exists with owner-scoped RLS but no UI. **Verdict: (b)** — state it as the next step in the pitch and README; do not build more before Sunday. The hash link already answers "can I share this comparison", judges cannot tell a slug from a hash, and anonymous auth plus RLS plus share UI plus offline degrade is runtime risk no test covers. Minimal next step, if wanted later: `tracts` (TractProps jsonb + geom, keyed by geoid, stamped with `scoring_version`), `scoring_configs`, `policy_areas`, `scenarios.hash`; no PostGIS (the client wants whole layers as GeoJSON and the only spatial op runs in the browser); direct selects under RLS, one `save_scenario` RPC; the flip map stays client-side. If forced: ~5 h.
- **Ten engineering fixes** (paths in `_agentC-architecture.md`): defer map creation one frame so the rail paints first (S); WebGL error state (S); keep Explore and Analysis mounted with `hidden` + `map.resize()` (M); intro frame-rate guard and a 3 s flight (S); Supabase retry plus a visible chip instead of `console.info` (S); basemap fallback after 2 s and lazy DEM sources (M); a data-contract test JSON ↔ types (S); tests for `remote.ts`, `geocode.ts`, `derived.ts`, `paint.ts` and `check:export` in `package.json` (M); `role="switch"`, `radiogroup`, a global `:focus-visible`, `aria-activedescendant` (S); stale counts, the dead `/data/` header, copy-link over `file://` (S).
- **Demo-day checklist:** run from the Vercel build or the file, never `npm run dev`; open the file once on the judge's laptop beforehand so the intro-skip flag and the tile cache exist; keep Terrain off on the projector; if "Loading map…" lasts more than 7 s the network is blocking openfreemap.org.

## Part 6 — Verdict

**Scores (1–5).**

| Criterion | Score | Why |
