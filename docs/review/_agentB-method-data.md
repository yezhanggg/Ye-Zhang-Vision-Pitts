## 1. Method critique

### 1.1 Structural findings (these matter more than any single cell)
1. **The subsidy flag is a switch, not a factor.** Balanced winners by `subsidy_eligible`: flag = 0 (36 tracts) → townhome 28, ADU 8, senior 0, small apt 0; flag = 1 (78 tracts) → senior 40, small apt 35, townhome 3, ADU 0. A 0/1 input with fits 1.0/0.8 against 0.0–0.2 decides the *family* of answer; the other five factors only pick within it. Halving the fits (0.5/0.5) does not fix it (senior still 42). The flag itself must be graded (see §4).
2. **Senior wins because nothing measures seniors.** The senior row (`need .6, market 0, displacement .2, subsidy .8, transit .6, flood −1`) is the least demanding row, so it is the default wherever the flag is on and the market is weak (24 of the 38 weak-tercile tracts). Both remedies in `docs/assumptions.md` §2 fail: senior market 0.15 → senior 40→37 (5 flips); senior flood −0.5 → senior **57** (17 flips), i.e. worse. Adding one demand signal that already sits in the bundle (`app/src/data/acs_tract.json` `age65_share`, percentile-ranked, fit senior 0.8 / others 0) gives senior 30, small apt 44, ADU 9, townhome 31 under Balanced (20 flips). Caveat: North Oakland reads 45% 65+ (a facility tract).
3. **Duplex/triplex can never win** (0 of 114 under Balanced; docs F-2). I tested a generous row (`subsidy .5, market .2, need .7`): still 0. No fit-matrix edit rescues it because duplex is strongest on nothing the tool measures: by-right zoning (R2/R3/RM), vacant and city-owned lots, existing 2–4 unit stock (`units_2_4_share` is in the Explore bundle but not scored). The typology Pittsburgh's land bank and CDCs actually build is structurally excluded.
4. **`need` is student-dominated at the top.** Highest `need_count`: North Oakland 1,005, South Oakland 855, Central Oakland 835 (`factor_methods.md` §3.1 admits this). South Oakland (need pct .99) gets **senior housing** as its Balanced pick. That is the first thing a Pitt-area planner will notice. ACS undergraduate enrollment (e.g. B14007) is a 30-minute flag.
5. **Transit per land acre penalizes brownfield tracts.** Hazelwood 5623 has 6,580 weekday departures on ~770 acres (Hazelwood Green and hillside inside the tract) → 8.5/acre → pct .25; Squirrel Hill North has 5,826 on ~233 acres → pct .61. Per household the order reverses (Hazelwood .60, Squirrel Hill North .54, Homewood North .82). Normalize by households or by the share of housing units within 400 m of a stop.
6. **Flood is the least credible factor and carries the strongest aversions (−1).** `flood_share_pct`: Shadyside 42003070500 79.6%, Homewood South 75.5%, Homewood North 31.8%. No Pittsburgh resident recognizes those. It is tagged `high` confidence in 114/114 tracts (R-3 is right to propose medium; the fix is FEMA NFHL, §3).
7. **Anti-displacement preset is mis-named for what it does.** In Squirrel Hill North, tripling displacement weight makes *low* risk the largest contributor to townhome (0.317 of 0.669). "Build market-rate where nobody is displaced" is defensible; "Protect current residents first" (`PRESET_COPY`) is not what the weights do. F-1 already knows; put F-1's sentence on screen.

### 1.3 Rulebook R1–R12 and the two cells without a rule (`handoff/assumptions.md` §0)
- R7 lumps small apartment (.4) and senior (.2) under "gentle density tolerates rising pressure": a 5–19 unit building is not gentle density and does not "add without removing anyone" on an occupied site. Split the rule.
- R10's rationale is inverted (above). R3's "market 0" and R4's "need .2" have no stated magnitude logic. R11 sets cells for a factor that does not exist in v0.3.0 (D2 PROPOSED); the rulebook mixes shipped and proposed cells and should say which.
- The two unruled cells (ADU × need .3, small apt × market .3): write the rules, do not zero them. Zeroing changes 19 Balanced picks (senior 31, small apt 42, ADU 14) as a side effect, not a decision. Proposed text: "an ADU carries no land cost, so it is the one market unit that can rent near 50% AMI: need .3"; "a 5–19 unit building carries a first mortgage sized on market rents even with LIHTC: market .3".
- Consistency: `docs/assumptions.md` §2 says "73 eligible tracts", handoff F-3 says "82 of 128", the data says **78** (`subsidy_eligible == 1`); watch list is 9 in docs §6 and the app, "12" in handoff C-8. Judges cross-check docs against the app.

### 1.4 Is the data/value separation legible on screen?
Yes at the frame level (header "value judgments are labeled", `Your values` pill, compare-scenarios "Your judgment calls, not data", Sources modal). No at the point of decision: the best-match card (screenshot 03) says "Small apartment · 74 / 100 match" and "Why: many low-income renters · subsidy eligibility" — the first phrase is an observation, the second is a fit rule, and nothing marks the difference. `matchText` reads like a measurement. The fit matrix is visible only inside Sources and is not editable in the UI (B11 unshipped), so a judge cannot test a cell without editing JSON. The map legend "Best match score" does not say "under your priorities". Fix is copy, not code: "74 / 100 fit under *your* priorities" and "Why (fit rule): …".

### 1.5 Would the uncertainty shown change what a planner does? Mostly not, for four reasons
1. Three of six tags are constant: `displacement_risk_conf` = low in **114/114** (R-5), `transit_access_conf` = high 114/114, `flood_exposure_conf` = high 114/114. `need` is low in 42, `market` low in 14. A tag that says the same thing everywhere is ignored after the second tract, and `DataLimitsPanel` prints "Low confidence: displacement risk" on every card.
2. Stability perturbs weights only (Dirichlet, κ = 25). Data uncertainty never reaches the pick: the 42 tracts with need CV > 30% get the same "Solid pick" as anyone.
3. "Stays #1 in N of 10" at 200 draws has a standard error of about a third of a step. Python gives Hazelwood/Balanced 0.45, the TS engine 0.455 with seed 0, and the app (per-tract seed, `derived.ts` line 46) shows 6 of 10 (screenshot 05). Engines agree; the digit does not. The parity fixture has no stability cases (`scoring_cases.json` keys: name, x, weights, scores). Set `stability_draws` to 1000 (config only) or show only the solid/likely/close word.
4. 49 of 114 tracts have a #1–#2 margin under 0.05 (23 under 0.02; median 0.056), and the "Which type wins" map paints all of them solid. A hatch for close calls would change what a planner takes to a meeting; nothing else in the panel would.
Confidence never changes a score (R-4) is the right rule; but then the tag must vary and the close-call must be visible.

### 1.6 F-2 and T-3
- F-2 (senior 40/114): the honest reading is that the tool reproduces the PHFA pipeline's path of least resistance (senior LIHTC: less opposition, less parking, easy lease-up) rather than informing it. Root cause is §1.1.1–1.1.2, not the "short row"; the row-normalized formula is fine.
- T-3 (ADU × 1.5 = no-op): confirmed, 0 flips. The proposed score multiplier (×1.1 → 12 flips … ×1.4 → 77) is a knob with no data behind it; do not ship it. ADU-by-right only means something *relative* to the other four types being conditional or prohibited in the same district (R1 districts: Squirrel Hill North). That is a per-typology feasibility gate from the zoning map (§3, #1), not a fit change.

**Hazelwood.** Half-defensible. **Homewood North.** Not defensible as stated. Weakest market in the city (raw .029): a 5–19 unit new-build does not pencil with LIHTC at that scale (9% deals in Pittsburgh run 40–60 units for soft-cost reasons); the realistic products are rehab of the 2–4 unit stock, scattered-site infill on land-bank lots and, yes, a larger senior or family LIHTC building on an assembled site. The tool's #1 is the wrong scale and its #3 (duplex .480) is the right one but can never win. Then "32% of the land floods": Homewood has real stormwater trouble but no floodplain a resident would recognize; that number would end the meeting. **Squirrel Hill North.** Defensible as a market statement, not as an affordability one. "Townhome 72, ADU 61, 10 of 10" is stable and true to the MVA; but there is no subsidy designation, no IZ overlay (Lawrenceville/Bloomfield/Polish Hill/Oakland only), and a market townhome serves no ≤50% AMI renter. The only sub-market unit that gets built here is an ADU on a large R1 lot, by right since the 2025 package, and the tool ranks it second because townhome × market is 1.0 and ADU × market 0.5. A Squirrel Hill Urban Coalition audience would also ask why "displacement risk low" when the CHAS burden share among ≤50% AMI renters is .79 (SVI .14 dominates the composite) and vouchers are suppressed. Missing: a by-right gate (townhome is not by-right in R1D), an affordability mechanism per typology (bonus / IZ / ADU rent), and the `age65_share` context.
